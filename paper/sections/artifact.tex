\section{Implementation and Artifact}

The companion artifact is available in the public repository%
\footnote{\url{https://github.com/VincenzoImp/encrypted-local-tally-integrity}.}.
The repository contains a Solidity contract that registers authorities,
verifies Groth16 proofs on-chain, fixes the encrypted-tally transcript,
maintains the aggregate ciphertext, and anchors both the trustee setup and the
final opening transcript. It also contains three Circom circuits~\cite{circom}: one for
encrypted local tally validity, one for trustee-registry consistency with the
election public key, and one for partial decryption share validity.

The end-to-end replay generates real proofs for all three stages, submits
signed encrypted local tallies, reconstructs the aggregate from the stored
transcript, checks that this reconstruction matches the on-chain aggregate, and
recovers the final tally.

The artifact's reviewer entry point is \texttt{yarn validate}. That
command first rebuilds the circuit artifacts if needed, then runs the contract
test suite, and finally executes the end-to-end replay. The canonical replay
scenario instantiates a two-candidate election with a $2$-of-$3$ trustee
committee and a dealer-generated threshold key. It contains three authorities
with electorate sizes $20$, $24$, and $20$, and hidden local tallies $(12,8)$,
$(9,15)$, and $(14,6)$ respectively. The resulting global tally is $(35,29)$,
so the winner is Alice.

The most important property the artifact demonstrates is that the local tallies
are never published in clear. What becomes public is only the accepted
ciphertext transcript, the fact that each ciphertext passed a well-formedness
proof and a signed submission check, the trustee-setup transcript, the verified
decryption-share transcript, and the final aggregate after threshold
decryption.
